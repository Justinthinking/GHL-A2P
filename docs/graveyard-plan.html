The Graveyard

Fix before you film

  1. Source every stat. The 42 hours, 78%, 80% drop, $25,000 and $76,500 figures need a citation you can put on screen. If you can't find a source, cut the number.
  2. Only real results count as proof. "312 estimates, $54,000 recovered" and "I audited a 3-truck roofer in Tyler" are placeholders until a real client produces them. Presenting made-up results as real breaks FTC endorsement rules and destroys trust if someone checks.
  3. Match the offer to the stack plan. Hour 5 pitches "$0 upfront, 12% of closed jobs." STACK.md Revision 2 uses a small setup fee plus a percentage. Pick one before recording the Package clips.
Section 1

Branding strategy

We're building a brand that people live inside, not just a reactivation service. Each principle below has the specific move it becomes for The Graveyard.

Build an ecosystem

A brand is an ongoing exchange of energy with the consumer, not a one-off product.

Graveyard: content, door knocks and campaigns all feed the same story.

Establish the room

The everyday setting your brand keeps coming back to.

Graveyard: the stark black-and-white whiteboard.

Social house codes

Rules that make the content recognizable without a logo.

Graveyard: hand-drawn tombstones, tally marks, the clock, the gear.

One core rant

A single argument you can make forever.

Graveyard: slow response and no follow-up are killing the business.

Trigger cortisol

Make the viewer feel exactly where they stand and that they're falling behind.

Graveyard: put a dollar figure on the estimate folder.

Shift the identity

Answer one core question and move the viewer into a new identity.

Graveyard: from "contractor losing jobs" to "owner who works every lead."

Lock in emotionally

People buy on emotion first, so anchor every piece in one feeling or daydream.

Graveyard: the relief of money coming back without new ad spend.

Build backwards

Start from the content and the consumer's world, then shape the product around it.

Graveyard: the content strategy leads, and the offer follows.

Section 2

The daily 6-hour batch

Record every whiteboard video in one 6-hour block, one piece at a time. Batching keeps the room looking identical, and the pieces get assembled in editing. Every script follows the same six beats:

  1. Pinch
  2. Agitate
  3. Reframe
  4. Solve
  5. Proof
  6. Package
  1. Hour 10:00–1:00

    Setup and strategy review

    • Set up the whiteboard, camera, lighting and markers.
    • Review the target pain points and script the six beats.
    Output: day's scripts
  2. Hour 21:00–2:00

    Cortisol triggers and stat hits

    • Record 10–15 short "Stat Hits," 30–45 seconds each.
    • Pinch only: use data to show they're losing money slowly.
    Output: 10–15 shorts
  3. Hour 32:00–3:00

    Story hooks

    • Film the openings for the Breakdown videos.
    • Record several versions of a contractor story (e.g. "Last week, I audited a 3-truck roofer in Tyler…"). Use real audits only.
    Output: hook variants
  4. Hour 43:00–4:00

    Whiteboard solves

    • Film the Reframe and Solve beats.
    • Draw the flowcharts, the gear (the system) and the tally marks (jobs recovered). Show the owner's transformation live on the board.
    Output: solve segments
  5. Hour 54:00–5:00

    Proof and package

    • Record the closing offers and case studies.
    • Pitch the Graveyard Sprint (offer terms to be confirmed; see "Fix before you film").
    Output: closers + case studies
  6. Hour 65:00–6:00

    Signature B-roll and door knocks

    • Film the signature hand-knocking B-roll, with no face shown.
    • Record the "Three Questions" door-knock framework as the visual hook for outreach content.
    Output: B-roll library
Section 3

Story-driven content framework

Every video tells the story of helping one contractor, backed by hard data and a real pain point.

  1. The story
    "I sat down with a roofing owner in East Texas who swore his leads were just bad. I asked to see his estimate folder." Template
  2. The data
    "The average roofer takes 42 hours to respond to a new lead. But 78% of customers choose the first contractor who responds." Source needed
  3. The pain
    "After 5 minutes, conversion drops 80%. By the time he was calling people back, his next $25,000 roof was already gone." Source needed
  4. The cost
    "Responding in 4 hours instead of 5 minutes costs roughly $76,500 a month. That's an estimate graveyard." Source needed
  5. The solve
    "I pulled 312 of his dead estimates. We ran a 3-step email reactivation sequence. He didn't need to spend another dime on marketing. He needed a shovel, and we dug up $54,000." Replace with real results
Section 4

Execution rules

Never leave the room

Every video is shot in the same place. No car, no desk. Stay at the whiteboard.

Consistency beats talent

Steady flow and a steady story outperform raw talent or a single viral hit. Stick to the posting schedule.

Control the consumer's journey

Show the daily habits you want contractors to adopt, not just the product, so the brand becomes part of their day.
